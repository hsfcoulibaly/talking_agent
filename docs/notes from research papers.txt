1st paper 

AI & Interactive Storytelling in Cultural Heritage
https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11227644


A prototype system was developed for the Dunhuang Murals (specifically the Jataka stories) to move beyond passive viewing

Core Modules: The system includes mural segmentation to explain individual elements, interactive knowledge graphs that trigger cinematic visual effects when clicked, and a creative reconstruction tool where users build their own comic-style narratives

AI Integration: An AI agent (built on the COZE platform and GPT models) acts as an approachable guide, providing real-time explanations and creative suggestions while drawing from a curated knowledge base to prevent "hallucinations"

Outcomes: User testing showed high scores in attractiveness, novelty, and stimulation, suggesting the system helps users form deeper emotional and intellectual connections with heritage

2nd paper
Virtual Reality (VR) for Prehistoric Archaeology
https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11025303

The Crvena Stijena VR project in Montenegro addresses the difficulty common visitors have in understanding prehistoric sites, which often appear as abstract excavations.

The Experience: Users enter a 3D reconstruction of the cave where they can explore digital replicas of archaeological findings. The application features an intro animation of the earth's creation and a "Lara Croft"-style guide.

Gamification: To increase engagement, users participate in a deer hunting gameplay using prehistoric tools, but only after passing a quiz on the educational content.

Key Findings: The most impressive part for users was the ability to handle and rotate 3D reconstructions of tool fragments, providing a level of access impossible in physical museums where items are behind glass

3rd paper Storytelling & AI in Engineering Education
https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9929609

Researchers at Shahid Beheshti University utilized storytelling and AI to teach complex engineering concepts, specifically series DC-DC converters, in online environments.

Metaphorical Learning: Technical circuit components were taught using metaphors; for example, a diode was described as a "Toll Road" (one-way traffic), and a sliding controller was cast as "Municipal Police".

Interactive Steps: The method involves five steps including questioning, student grouping in Adobe Connect, and a creative phase where students draw diagrams based on the story metaphors.

AI's Role: Students were required to compare their own technical interpretations of the story with AI-generated responses to promote deeper critical analysis.

Outcomes: Over 90% of students found the method more effective and memorable than "dry" traditional lectures, noting it significantly increased participation in online settings
